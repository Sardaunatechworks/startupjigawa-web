1. Executive Summary
Startup Jigawa Ltd is a technology and digital innovation company based in Dutse, Jigawa State, Nigeria, incorporated under RC 7256149. Over nine years of active operation, the company has grown from a grassroots technology and innovation hub into an institution delivering digital skills training, technology products, research and civic-technology services across Northern Nigeria.
In that time, Startup Jigawa has trained over 50,000 people in digital and technical skills, delivered programmes in partnership with the Jigawa State Government and federal agencies including NITDA, the Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, 3MTT and the Nigeria Jubilee Fellows Programme, and worked alongside international and civil-society partners including JICA, the Open Government Partnership (OGP) Jigawa, and a network of civil society organizations across the country.
The company operates across five core development sectors — Agricultural Technology, Health Technology, Educational Technology, Government Technology, and Digital Commerce & Entrepreneurship — and is extending its work into civic technology, social accountability and open government, building on nine years of direct experience delivering technology to real communities, institutions and public-sector partners.
This profile sets out Startup Jigawa's legal status, history, governance, programme areas, partnerships, operating principles, impact-measurement approach, financial sustainability model and forward strategy, to support due diligence by government agencies, development partners, investors and civil-society collaborators.

22. Our Delivery Model: Community-to-Policy Pathway
Startup Jigawa applies a consistent delivery model across its sectors and civic-technology work, linking community-level insight to technology, evidence and, where relevant, policy engagement:
Community Listening  →  Problem Documentation  →  Data Collection  →  Analysis  →  Community Validation  →  Policy / Solution Design  →  Digital Prototype or Civic Intervention  →  Pilot  →  Evaluation  →  Scale or Policy Dialogue
Illustrative Walkthrough
This pathway is best understood through how it would typically be applied — for example, in a civic-technology or public-service intervention:
—	1. Community Listening — Facilitators and field associates engage directly with community members, traders, farmers or civil servants to surface a specific, recurring problem. 
—	2. Problem Documentation — The problem is documented in a structured brief, distinguishing it from anecdote or assumption. 
—	3. Data Collection & Analysis — A short survey, focus-group or administrative-data exercise is conducted to quantify the scale and character of the problem. 
—	4. Community Validation — Findings are shared back with the affected community or institution to confirm they reflect lived reality before a solution is designed. 
—	5. Solution Design — Based on validated evidence, the team designs a digital tool, service adjustment or policy recommendation appropriate to the problem and to local infrastructure constraints. 
—	6. Pilot — A working version is tested with a limited group of real users under real conditions, not only in a lab setting. 
—	7. Evaluation — Pilot results are assessed against the original problem statement to determine whether the intervention worked, needs adjustment, or should be discontinued. 
—	8. Scale or Policy Dialogue — Where a pilot is validated, it is scaled to additional communities or institutions, or, where appropriate, used to inform a policy conversation with government partners. 
This model is what links Startup Jigawa's technology-delivery capacity with its research and civic-engagement role, and is applied consistently whether the starting point is an agricultural advisory gap, a school-connectivity problem, or a citizen-feedback need.
